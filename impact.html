Project Second Voice Speaks at National Home Is Here Convening

November 18, 2026

70

participants at the convening

Project Second Voice founder and United We Dream Southwest regional core leader Jonathan Wang spoke at “The Fight for the Dream and Promise Act,” a national virtual community convening organized by United We Dream and Home Is Here, a coalition working to protect DACA recipients, their families, and immigrant communities through advocacy and collective action. United We Dream’s Senior Political Director, Bruna Bouhid-Sollod, personally invited Jonathan to speak.

The audience included advocates from multiple organizations. Jonathan shared Michelle Medina’s story, drawing on his interviews and ongoing conversations with her through Project Second Voice. Michelle came to the United States at age four, grew up in San Antonio, Texas, and earned a bachelor’s degree in criminal justice. After spending 25 days in immigration detention, she was released on bond. She described the financial and emotional uncertainty of navigating immigration proceedings while rebuilding her life.

Jonathan connected Michelle’s hopes for a career, stability for her family, and a secure future to the need for permanent protections and a pathway to citizenship for Dreamers. He emphasized that sharing individual experiences can help congressional representatives understand what is at stake for immigrant communities.

The convening took place during the post-election lame-duck session, before the next Congress took office in January 2027. At the time of the event, the Dream and Promise Act discharge petition had 217 signatures. Organizers were seeking the 218th signature needed to advance the discharge process toward House consideration.

Alongside United We Dream organizers, Jonathan helped mobilize participants to contact their congressional representatives and urge them to sign the petition before the term ended. The convening connected Project Second Voice’s immigrant storytelling with coordinated national advocacy.

Los Angeles, California

June 23, 2026

Invited by United We Dream to represent the organization as a youth leader and founder of Project Second Voice during a congressional advocacy meeting with the staff of Congressman Brad Sherman (California's 32nd Congressional District).

During the meeting, I shared one of the immigrant stories collected through Project Second Voice to highlight the real-world impact that immigration policies have on individuals and families. The discussion focused on current immigration challenges, including:

  • DACA processing delays
  • Temporary Protected Status (TPS)
  • The Dream and Promise Act

This meeting demonstrated how community storytelling can directly inform policy discussions and ensure that lived experiences are represented in conversations with congressional offices.

Beyond the meeting itself, one meaningful outcome was the opportunity to connect a Project Second Voice community member who had been personally affected by DACA processing delays with support through United We Dream. After sharing her story, I was able to introduce her to the appropriate United We Dream team so she could access guidance and resources regarding her situation.

This experience reinforced Project Second Voice's mission of not only amplifying immigrant voices but also helping connect community members with organizations and resources that can support them.

The congressional seal is used solely as a visual identifier for the congressional meeting. This milestone summary does not imply endorsement by Congressman Brad Sherman, the United States Congress, or the U.S. House of Representatives.
